CASE STUDY 01 · INFORMATION MANAGEMENT

Protection Information Needs Mapping and Partner Capacity Strengthening

Turning an information needs resource into a practical coordination and capacity-strengthening exercise.

Information mapping is most useful when partners can apply it to reporting, coordination and decision-making.

My contribution to the Protection Information Needs Catalogue included information mapping and capacity strengthening for protection partners across Northwest Nigeria.

I trained protection partners on the information needs resource and ActivityInfo, supporting a shared understanding of what information is needed, how it can be organised and how digital systems can support consistent information management.

My contribution: partner training, information mapping, technical guidance and ActivityInfo capacity development.

CASE STUDY 02 · PROTECTION INFORMATION MANAGEMENT

Information Landscape and Data Governance for National Protection Analysis

Connecting information sources, analytical priorities and responsible data practices.

A credible analysis depends on knowing what information exists, who stewards it and how it can be used responsibly.

Contributing to the design of an information landscape and data governance framework for national protection analysis through the Protection Monitoring and Analysis Cell (PMAC) and Protection Advocacy Group (PAG).

The work involves identifying and organising relevant information sources, understanding how information moves between actors and considering the roles, standards and safeguards needed to support coherent analysis. It links information mapping with practical governance questions, including data quality, access, stewardship, confidentiality and responsible use.

My contribution: information landscape development, source mapping, data governance thinking and support to coordinated protection analysis.

PMAC / PAGInformation landscapeData governanceProtection analysis

CASE STUDY 03 · INFORMATION SYSTEMS & DATA QUALITY

Strengthening programme information systems and data quality.

Making routine information more consistent, accessible and useful for programme decisions.

Good information management is not simply about collecting more data; it is about making information reliable, usable and appropriately protected.

Across humanitarian and development programmes, I have supported the design and use of information processes that connect data collection, validation, analysis and reporting.

This includes working with digital platforms such as ActivityInfo and KoboToolbox, structuring forms and information flows, applying data quality checks, developing dashboards and maps, and helping teams understand how to maintain accurate records. In protection information management, these practices also require attention to confidentiality, role-based access and responsible handling of sensitive information.

My contribution: digital information workflows, data quality assurance, user guidance, dashboards, mapping and practical capacity strengthening.

CASE STUDY 04 · RESEARCH, MEAL & LEARNING

Research and evidence for adaptive programming.

From assessment design and field research to analysis, learning and programme improvement.

Evidence matters when it helps teams understand what is changing, for whom and what needs to happen next.

My MEAL and research experience spans education and humanitarian programming, including baseline studies, situational analyses, midline and endline evaluations, field monitoring and learning activities.

I have supported quantitative and qualitative data collection through surveys, focus group discussions and key informant interviews; trained enumerators and programme teams; conducted data verification and quality assurance; and translated findings into reports, dashboards and practical recommendations. This includes work under the USAID LEARN to Read and USAID OTL projects.

My contribution: research and assessment tools, field team capacity development, mixed-methods data collection, analysis, reporting and evidence use.
